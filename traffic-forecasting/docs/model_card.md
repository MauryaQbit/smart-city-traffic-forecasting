# Model Card

Traffic forecasting models that predict hourly vehicle counts at four city
junctions. Based on the [Model Cards for Model Reporting (Mitchell et al.,
2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03907) structure.

## Model details

- **Organization:** Project 9, ML Internship.
- **Person / team training:** intern deliverable.
- **Date:** 2026-08.
- **Version:** `traffic-forecast 0.1.0`.
- **Type:** four parallel models, registered side by side.

| Model | Family | Implementation | Hyperparameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear Regression | linear | `sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression` | (none) |
| Random Forest (default) | ensemble | `sklearn.ensemble.RandomForestRegressor` | `n_estimators=100` |
| Random Forest (tuned) | ensemble | same | grid search winner: `n_estimators=400, max_depth=20, min_samples_leaf=1, max_features='sqrt'` |
| SARIMA | classical time series | `statsmodels.tsa.statespace.SARIMAX` | `order=(1,0,1)`, `seasonal_order=(1,1,1,24)`, fit on trailing 2 months per junction |
| LSTM | recurrent neural net | custom PyTorch 2-layer LSTM (32 hidden) | Adam lr=2e-3, batch=512, epochs<=15, grad clip 1.0, early stop patience 4, seq_len=24 |

## Intended use

- **Primary use:** educational / portfolio piece. Demonstrates a four-model
  comparison on hourly time-series data with a proper train/val split,
  feature engineering, and an interactive dashboard.
- **Out-of-scope uses:** real-time traffic control, signal timing, routing.
  The dataset is synthetic. Even on real data, none of these models is
  calibrated for safety-critical decisions.

## Training data

- **Source:** `data/raw/traffic_data.csv`, generated by
  `tf-generate-data` (`traffic_forecast.data.generate.build_dataset`).
- **Span:** 2022-01-01 00:00 to 2023-12-31 23:00 (2 years of hourly data).
- **Junctions:** 4, with junction 1 the busiest (mean volume ~55) and
  junction 4 the quietest (~10).
- **Behavior:** two-peak daily shape (morning + evening rush), weekday/weekend
  split, mild monsoon dip (June-September), holiday traffic reduction to 55%
  of normal, 0.3% spike rate, 0.1% missing values injected for realism.
- **Train/val split:** chronological 80/20. Train spans 2022-01-08 00:00 to
  2023-08-09 08:00 (the first 168h per junction are dropped to give weekly
  lag features full context); val spans 2023-08-09 09:00 to 2023-12-31 23:00
  (~20.6 weeks). No shuffle - time-respecting.

## Evaluation data

Same schema as training, last ~20.6 weeks of timestamps per the chronological
split. 69,408 engineered rows total -> 55,524 train / 13,884 val after the
168-hour lag-context drop.

SARIMA is evaluated on a smaller window: first 2 weeks of validation per
junction (336 hours x 4 = 1,344 rows). This is disclosed in the dashboard's
"Methodology" expander and in the comparison table's `note` column.

## Metrics

Post-Foundation + sub-project #2 numbers, on the validation set:

| Model | MAE (vehicles/hr) | RMSE | Train time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random Forest (tuned) | 3.17 | 4.41 | ~85 s |
| LSTM | 3.19 | 4.65 | ~54 s |
| Random Forest (default) | 3.27 | 4.53 | ~4 s |
| Linear Regression | 3.66 | 5.01 | <0.1 s |
| SARIMA | 4.66 | 6.63 | ~27 s |

Mean vehicle count is ~20, so the winning models sit around 16% mean absolute
percentage error. RMSE > MAE across the board because errors are heavy-tailed
around rush-hour spikes.

## Ethical considerations

- **Synthetic data:** the generator is calibrated to look plausible but is
  not real traffic. Do not deploy this on real infrastructure.
- **Surveillance / privacy:** real traffic cameras and counters can be used
  to track individuals. If this pipeline is ever pointed at real data, prefer
  aggregate vehicle counts over identifiable vehicle data, and follow local
  surveillance disclosure rules.
- **Bias:** the synthetic generator embeds assumptions (e.g. holidays from
  the Indian public-holiday calendar, monsoon dip). On real data from
  elsewhere, seasonal and holiday features must be re-derived from local
  context.
- **Fairness across junctions:** error is concentrated at the busiest
  junction during rush hours (see dashboard tab 3). A deployment that
  prioritized low-traffic junctions (because they're "easier to predict")
  would be misleading.

## Caveats and recommendations

- The LSTM is sensitive to RNG seed despite `torch.manual_seed(42)`.
  Re-running stage 3 produces MAE within +-0.1 of the reported number; tighten
  characterization bands if you need stricter reproducibility.
- SARIMA's "trailing 2-month fit" is a runtime compromise, not a methodology
  recommendation. Full-history SARIMA at hourly resolution with seasonal
  period 24 is prohibitively slow. For real deployment, refit weekly.
- The Random Forest pickle is 60 MB. For deployment on RAM-constrained hosts
  (Streamlit Community Cloud free tier), reduce `n_estimators` before retraining.

## Maintenance

- **Reproducibility:** `tf-train-all` from a clean checkout reproduces the
  numbers within the bands enforced by `tests/characterization/`.
- **Drift monitoring:** not implemented. If the real dataset is ever swapped
  in, add a check that the engineered-frame column means are within a
  tolerance of the synthetic frame, otherwise model performance will degrade
  silently.
